## Episode format: Brief

This is a short episode for a listener who has five to seven minutes. It gives the thesis, the two or three ideas
that matter most, and one thing to do. The skill of this format is leaving things out: choose the ideas that carry
the argument, and do not try to cover the source.

### Beats, in order
1. Cold open: one striking line, short.
2. Intro and setup, together and brief: welcome, what the source is and who made it, in one or two exchanges, and the
   expert introduced.
3. Key ideas (two to three): each is one host question, one expert answer built on one or two pieces of the plan's
   evidence, and a one-line restated takeaway from the host. No extended follow-up chains.
4. The catch: one exchange in which the host raises the single strongest caveat or objection and the expert answers it
   honestly. It sits after the key ideas and replaces the standard turn.
5. Practical close: the one thing to do, in one or two exchanges.
6. Outro: at most two takeaways, and sign-off.

### Plan shape (planner)
Two to three main segments, each with the standard segment layout but with one or two evidence items, not more. One
cold open candidate is enough and a minimal setup. The plan names "The catch" where the standard plan has "The turn".
You may rely on the digest to decide which ideas carry the argument, but every line you cite must still be checked
against the source text, as for any plan. A short plan is not a licence for vague evidence: each item still carries
its specifics and its citation.

### Host and expert
- Keep the host persona's voice and rules: short turns, one question at a time, concrete over abstract. Skip the
  extended techniques (follow-up chains, repeated recaps); a brief does not have room for them.
- The expert answers in a few sentences, plainly, with the one example that makes the idea land.

### Length
About 800 to 1,100 words of dialogue. Do not pad to reach the length, and do not exceed 1,200 words.

### How reviewers judge this format
- Structure: use the beats above, not the standard beats. There is no separate turn: the catch is one exchange. Two
  to three key ideas are required, not four to six. Flag a script over about 1,200 words, or a segment that sprawls.
- Depth: do not ask for second examples, extra mechanisms or exhaustive coverage. Ask only that each key idea is
  explained in plain words with its one piece of evidence, and that the most important ideas of the source were chosen.
  Missing secondary material is not a finding in this format.
- Fidelity: unchanged. A short episode makes fewer claims, and every one is checked against the source.
