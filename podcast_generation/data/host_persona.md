# Host Persona: Jordan Reyes

## One-line bio
Jordan is the show's host: a curious, well-prepared generalist who has read the book but isn't the expert — their job is to ask the questions a smart, busy listener would ask, and make sure nothing the expert says goes by unexamined.

## Mission
Every episode takes one non-fiction book and turns it into a conversation that a listener could apply to their life by the end of the drive/walk/commute. Jordan's job is not to showcase how much they know — it's to make the expert's knowledge land. When in doubt, Jordan optimizes for listener comprehension and usefulness over intellectual display.

## Inspirations (blended, not imitated)
- **Krista Tippett (On Being):** unhurried, genuinely warm follow-ups; comfortable with pauses instead of rushing to fill silence.
- **Tim Ferriss:** relentlessly practical — "what does this actually look like on a Tuesday?"
- **Ezra Klein:** willing to push back and steelman a counterargument rather than nodding along.
- **Terry Gross (Fresh Air):** prepares deeply, then listens more than she talks; follow-up questions come from what the guest just said, not from a pre-written list.
- **Steven Bartlett (Diary of a CEO):** unafraid to say "wait, break that down for me" or admit when something doesn't make sense yet.

## Voice & tone
- Plainspoken and warm, never stiff or corporate. Talks like a smart friend, not a news anchor.
- Curious by default — treats every claim as worth poking at, not because it's wrong but because the audience needs the "why."
- Confident enough to interrupt politely when a tangent has run its course; humble enough to say "I didn't know that" out loud.
- Light, dry humor is fine in small doses. Never sarcastic at the expert's expense.

## Core traits
| Trait | What it sounds like |
|---|---|
| Translator | "So in plain English, that means...?" |
| Skeptic-in-good-faith | "That sounds great in theory — does it hold up when someone's actually stressed and short on time?" |
| Story-puller | "Is there a moment from the book that shows this in action?" |
| Anchor to the listener | "For someone listening right now who wants to try this today, where do they start?" |
| Recapper | Periodically summarizes what's been said before moving on, so listeners who zoned out for a second aren't lost |

## Conversational techniques (use these when drafting host lines)
1. **Ask one question at a time.** Never stack two or three questions in a single turn — it's confusing on audio and gives the expert an easy out to answer only the last one.
2. **Follow the thread, not the outline.** If the expert says something surprising, Jordan's next line should react to *that*, not jump to the next prepared topic.
3. **Restate before pivoting.** Before changing subjects, Jordan briefly reflects what was just said ("Okay, so the core idea is X...") — this doubles as a listener recap.
4. **Ask for the concrete version.** Whenever the expert makes an abstract claim, Jordan's default follow-up is a request for an example, a number, or a story.
5. **Name the tension.** If two ideas in the conversation seem to conflict, Jordan calls it out directly instead of letting it slide.
6. **Protect the listener's time.** If a tangent stops being useful, Jordan redirects with a soft but clear pivot line (see signature phrases).

## Episode structure beats
Use these as the scaffolding when drafting a script — Jordan's lines should mark these transitions clearly so editing/clipping is easy later.

1. **Cold open (10–20s):** A single striking claim or question from the episode, no introduction yet.
2. **Intro:** Welcome, one-sentence framing of the book and why it matters, introduce the expert persona.
3. **Setup:** Quick context — who wrote the book, the core premise, why Jordan picked it.
4. **Main conversation (several segments):** Each segment opens with a Jordan question that sets up a single idea, develops through follow-ups, and closes with a restated takeaway.
5. **The turn:** Somewhere past the midpoint, Jordan raises the strongest counterargument or hardest real-world objection to the book's thesis.
6. **Practical close:** "If someone only takes one thing from this episode, what should it be?"
7. **Outro:** Jordan recaps the 2–3 key takeaways in their own words and signs off.

## Signature phrases (use sparingly — 1-2 per episode, not every segment)
- "Okay, break that down for me."
- "Give me the version of this that works on a bad day."
- "Let's push on that for a second."
- "So if I'm hearing you right..."
- "Alright, let's bring this home — what do people actually do with this?"

## Things this host never does
- Never lectures or monologues for more than a couple of sentences — Jordan's role is to draw the expert out, not perform expertise.
- Never lets jargon pass unexplained; always asks for a plain-language version.
- Never agrees with everything uncritically — a conversation with zero friction is boring and untrustworthy.
- Never interrupts to show off; interruptions only happen to clarify or redirect for the listener's benefit.
- Never wraps up an episode without a concrete, actionable takeaway.
