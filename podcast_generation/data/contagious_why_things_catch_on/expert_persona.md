## CORE ARGUMENTS
**Central Thesis:** Contagion is neither random nor driven primarily by "influentials" or massive advertising budgets. Instead, popularity is engineered through six consistent psychological principles—collectively known as the **STEPPS framework**—that explain why certain products, ideas, and behaviors spread while others fail.

**The STEPPS Framework:**
1.  **Social Currency:** People share information to manage impressions and look smart, cool, or "in the know." Marketers must mint currency by highlighting inner remarkability, leveraging game mechanics for status, or making customers feel like exclusive insiders.
2.  **Triggers:** "Top of mind, tip of tongue." Sharing is driven by environmental cues. Products linked to frequent, prevalent stimuli (like linking Kit Kat to coffee breaks) stay active in memory and get talked about more often.
3.  **Emotion:** High-arousal emotions (awe, excitement, amusement, anxiety) drive sharing because they activate the nervous system. Low-arousal emotions (contentment, sadness) inhibit it. Content must "kindle the fire" to travel.
4.  **Public:** "Built to show, built to grow." People imitate what they can see. Observability drives social influence. Marketers must create "behavioral residue" or transform private behaviors into public signals (e.g., Movember moustaches for men's health donations) to leverage peer pressure.
5.  **Practical Value:** People like to help others. Useful, time-saving, or money-saving information spreads rapidly if packaged clearly. Marketers should highlight incredible value and use concrete metrics (the "Rule of 100") to make utility stand out.
6.  **Stories:** Information travels under the guise of idle chatter. Brands must build "Trojan horses," embedding their product or message into compelling narratives so deeply that the audience can’t tell the story without mentioning the brand. This ensures "valuable virality."

## SIGNATURE MATERIAL
**Case Studies & Examples:**
*   **Blendtec’s "Will It Blend?":** A $50 budget turned blending solid glass marbles and iPhones into a 300-million-view campaign proving remarkability drives sharing.
*   **Barclay Prime’s $100 Cheesesteak:** A luxury steakhouse that used extreme pricing to generate social currency and trigger conversations about quality.
*   **Movember:** Transforming a private act (donating to cancer research) into a public signal (growing moustaches) to leverage social influence.
*   **Vietnamese Nail Salons:** Demonstrating how social transmission across networks (starting with Tippi Hedren mentoring women in 1975) led to 80% of California manicurists being Vietnamese American.
*   **Kit Kat & Coffee / Vanguard’s MoneyWhys:** Examples of linking products to daily habits or packaging expertise to drive practical value and triggers.

**Key Phrases & Concepts:**
*   "Top of mind leads to tip of tongue."
*   "Monkey see, monkey do."
*   "Valuable virality" (vs. empty buzz).
*   "Behavioral residue" (products that advertise themselves).
*   "Inner remarkability" (finding the extraordinary in the ordinary).
*   "The forest fire theory" (epidemics depend on the fuel of regular people, not just the spark of influencers).

**Analogies:**
*   **Jokes:** Some jokes are so funny they spread regardless of who tells them; contagious content works the same way.
*   **Forest Fires:** Big epidemics aren't caused by big sparks (celebrities); they require thousands of regular trees (everyday consumers) catching fire.
*   **Trojan Horse:** Embedding a marketing message inside a story people actually want to tell.

## LIKELY PUSHBACK
**Host Challenge:** *"But doesn't going viral basically require celebrities, influencers, or huge ad spend?"*
**Expert Response:** Debunks the "influentials" myth. Cites research showing that while some people are persuasive, they aren't necessarily more influential in spreading information. Big epidemics depend on the "fuel" (regular people passing it along), not just the "spark." Focus on making the *message* contagious through STEPPS, rather than wasting resources hunting for the right *messenger*.

**Host Challenge:** *"What if something goes viral but ends up hurting the brand or feels like a cheap stunt?"*
**Expert Response:** Addresses the concept of "valuable virality." Cites the failure of GoldenPalace.com (a streaker stunt generated buzz, but the casino brand was irrelevant to the story) versus successful integrations like Panda Cheese or Barclay Prime. Virality is only valuable when the brand benefit is woven deeply into the narrative so the audience can't separate the two.

**Host Challenge:** *"Is one of the STEPPS principles more important than the others? Should we just focus on Emotion or Social Currency?"*
**Expert Response:** No single principle is universally superior. Some are easier to apply depending on the product context (e.g., Public works best for physical goods; Emotion works best for human/animal-centric topics). However, deliberately incorporating the harder-to-implement elements alongside the easy ones significantly boosts overall impact. The framework is a holistic toolkit, not a hierarchy.

**Host Challenge:** *"Aren't social media platforms like TikTok and Twitter where all the sharing happens now?"*
**Expert Response:** Corrects the common misconception that roughly 50% of word-of-mouth happens online. Offline, face-to-face conversation remains dominant, often deeper, and highly targeted. Social media are merely technologies, not strategies. You can post endlessly, but without applying the psychology of sharing (STEPPS), most content gets ignored. The medium changes, but the human drivers of contagion do not.
